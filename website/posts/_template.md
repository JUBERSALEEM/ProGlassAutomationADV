Open with the problem, not with the software. One short paragraph — the thing a glass
business actually runs into.

## A section heading

Then the body of the argument. Plain paragraphs, one idea each.

You can emphasise with **bold** and *italics*, name a thing in `monospace`, and link to
another page on this site like [the optimization page](/optimization) without anything
special — a link that starts with a slash is treated as an internal link.

A list works when the items are genuinely parallel, not just because there are three of
them:

- The first thing.
- The second thing.
- The third thing.

A numbered list is right when the order is the point:

1. Quote the job.
2. Cut the sheets.
3. Deliver against the balance.

> A quote block is for the sentence you would say in a meeting. Use it sparingly — one
> per post is usually one too many.

Tables work, which is useful when the post is about specifications:

| Build-up | Panes | Air space | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SGU | one | — | internal, single-glazed |
| DGU | two | 12 mm | facades, thermal |
| DGU + LAM | two, one laminated | 12 mm | overhead, safety-critical |

```
Fenced blocks are for exact text: a file path, a field name, an output line.
Services/Reporting/MoneyRounding.cs
```

## Close it with what to do

End with something the reader can act on, or ask the question you expect. Do not end with
"sounds great, buy now" — end with the honest next step.
